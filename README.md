### Programs Develop Programs

# The Open Source Origins of Hybrid Intelligence

Complementary materials for the chapter *"Programs develop programs: The open source origins of hybrid intelligence"* in the **Handbook of Hybrid Intelligence: Unlocking the Potential of Human–Machine Collaboration** (Springer Nature, 2026).

## About

Agentic AI systems have begun to collaborate much as humans do, marking a new era of _hybrid intelligence_: **the reciprocal collaboration of humans and machines**. The chapter examines how this era emerges and what sustains it. The premise is that hybrid intelligence has a material base: every capable model is trained on the digital commons, the shared record of code, text, data and media that people have accumulated over decades, such as open source code. 

To understand hybrid intelligence, the chapter therefore begins with the commons it feeds on. From there, it addresses a set of practical questions. What do models actually learn, from data and from people? What becomes of openness when the artefact being released is a model rather than source code? And what happens when machine output flows back into the very commons that future models must learn from? 

The answers are developed historically, tracing how software sharing became a platform-scale commons, and how that commons was later converted into machine-learning capability and a record now co-written by humans and machines in collaboration.

The chapter suggests that hybrid intelligence depends on the renewal of the commons that feeds it, and that designing for that renewal, through provenance, attribution, and returned value, deserves a central place on the field's agenda in the coming decades.
